# Wine etiquette in India: *the quiet rules of the business dinner.*

By [Arpita Mehta](https://arpitamehta.co/about.html) · 4 minute read · Updated May 2026

![Wine etiquette in India: order with confidence, hold it right. Infographic by Arpita Mehta.](https://arpitamehta.co/images/blog/wine-infographic-v2.jpg)

The wine list lands on the table. A small, quiet moment. How you handle the next thirty seconds tells the room something about every room you have ever been in.

## It is not about wine

It is about composure. The business dinner is never casual. Decisions about people are made across tables, and the person who handles the table fluently signals, without announcing it, that they are comfortable in rooms of consequence. The wine is the test. The dinner is the interview.

> "Holding a glass correctly will not close the deal. Holding it wrongly might lose it."

## Five things the table reads

- **The glass.** White and rosé held by the stem. Always. A warm hand changes the wine. The table notices before you do.
- **The order.** "What do you recommend?" is confident. Pointing silently at a number is the tell that says you would rather not be seen choosing.
- **The taste.** One honest sentence. No performance required. Silence with a nod is equally fluent. The only wrong answer is a fabricated one.
- **Your choice at the table.** Whether you drink or you do not, own it with the same ease as every other choice you make there. "I will stay with water tonight" said once, comfortably, needs no explanation and no apology. The composure is the same regardless of what is in the glass.
- **The clink.** Eye contact. Every time. Brief, direct, unhurried. In every culture and increasingly in every city in India, the eyes that look away are noticed.

## The honest truth

Fluency at a table is physical. It lives in the body, not the memory. A memorised rule is the first thing that leaves when a real dinner arrives with real stakes. What stays is practice, at an actual table, with someone who has done this long enough to make the learning feel like conversation.
