## The criterion nobody names

By the time your name reaches the table, your competence is already assumed. The question being asked is different: **can we put this person in a room and trust what happens next?** Decision makers answer it not from your appraisal file but from a thousand small impressions. That collection has a name. Nobody told you it was the actual criterion, because if they did they would have to admit they cannot teach it in a workshop.

> "Nobody promotes potential they cannot see. Make it visible."

## What the room actually reads

Five signals. Formed before you finish your first sentence.

- **Voice pace.** Rushed speech reads as nervous regardless of content. Authority lives slower.
- **Stillness.** The most senior person in any room always moves least. Fidgeting is a transmission the room receives whether you intend it or not.
- **The pause.** Two seconds before a hard answer reads as judgement. Answering instantly reads as eagerness. Judgement is what they are actually promoting.
- **A stated position.** Summarising what everyone said is helpful. Saying what you think should happen is promotable. There is one of each in every meeting.
- **Visual coherence.** Your appearance casts a vote before you speak. It should be voting for the role above yours.

Reading this and feeling a familiar sting is the point. The gap between knowing these signals and transmitting them under pressure is where executive presence lives, and where it is lost.
