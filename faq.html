The practice

A presence consultant works on the gap between who you are and how the world currently sees you. The work covers image, executive presence, social culture and AI for branding and business, because these things together are what a room actually reads when it reads you. Most people address one of them at a time with different professionals. This practice integrates all of them, because the rooms you walk into test all of them simultaneously.

Image is one of four pillars, not the whole practice. A stylist chooses clothes. This practice builds a visual identity, then layers executive presence, social fluency and strategic personal positioning on top. The result is not a better wardrobe. It is a person the room reads differently.

Most coaches work on mindset. Most stylists work on clothes. Most etiquette consultants work on rules. This practice works on all of it simultaneously, because the room experiences all of it simultaneously. The integration is the differentiator, and it comes from over ten years in marketing, a WSET qualification in wines, an engineering background and the specific ability to see how all four signals combine into a single impression.

How to begin

The Presence Quotient is a sixteen-question assessment that measures four dimensions: image, executive presence, social culture and distinction. It produces a score out of one hundred, a band and a private four-page PDF scorecard naming your biggest gap and what Arpita would prioritise if she were beginning with you. It is the fastest way to understand where you stand before any conversation, and most people find the result uncomfortably accurate.

It is self-reported, so it is as honest as you are. Most people find that answering carefully produces a result that matches what they have privately suspected for some time. It is designed to name things, not flatter them. If a result surprises you, that surprise itself is useful information.

Results and process

The results are specific to each person's gap and context, which is why no generic claim is made here. What clients consistently describe is a shift in how rooms receive them: being listened to more closely, being taken more seriously, and feeling that how they are seen finally matches who they are. Some get the promotion. Some get the client. Some simply stop dreading the rooms they used to shrink in.

Some signals shift in the first session. A change in voice pace, a new posture, a wardrobe decision made with intention rather than habit: these are felt immediately. The deeper changes, the ones that hold under pressure and in unfamiliar rooms, take longer. Sustainable change is the goal, not a performance that fades by the following week.
