The two rules that break things

1. Numbers cannot skip

True of testimonials/ and sessions/ alike. The page looks for 1, then 2, then 3, and stops at the first number it cannot find. A gap hides everything after it.

1.jpg 2.jpg 3.jpg 4.jpg all four appear 1.jpg 2.jpg 4.jpg only two appear, 4 is invisible

So when you delete one from the middle, renumber the rest to close the gap. The limit is 24, which you will not reach soon.

2. Replacing means the same name, exactly

Case and extension both count. Portrait.JPG is a different file from portrait.jpg as far as the site is concerned, and the page will show nothing.

Your About page is built to survive this: if the portrait is missing it quietly shows the monogram instead of a broken image. Useful, but it also means a failed upload looks deliberate rather than broken, so check the page afterwards.

Sizes that keep the site fast

Every image on the site today, added together, is under 3 MB. That is the reason pages feel instant, and it is worth protecting. A single unresized phone photo is often 4 to 8 MB on its own.

Why these numbers are bigger than they look. Phone and laptop screens pack two or three pixels into every one the page measures in, so a picture needs roughly twice its displayed size to look sharp rather than soft. The sizes below already account for that. The portrait on the homepage is a live example of getting it wrong: it is displayed 700 wide from an 800 wide file, which is barely 1x, so it is already slightly soft on a good laptop screen.

Use JPG for photographs. PNG only for the logo and anything needing a transparent background, because photographs saved as PNG come out several times larger for no visible gain.

Two notes for a professional shoot. Ask for full-resolution JPEGs rather than web exports, keep those as your archive, and resize copies down for the site. Large to small looks excellent; small to large is impossible.

And the homepage portrait slot is wide, not upright: an upright photo gets cropped hard to fit. Shoot something with room around you for that one, or pick a frame that survives losing its top and bottom.

Resizing without software: squoosh.app runs in the browser, is free, and shows you the before and after weight as you drag the quality slider. Drop the photo in, set the width from the table above, pull quality down until the file is under the target, and download. Quality around 75 is usually indistinguishable from the original.

How many is too many

The code stops looking after 24 in each folder, but that is not the number to aim for. Every photo in the strip loads on the homepage, and the strip is drawn twice so it can loop seamlessly. The homepage carries about 940 KB of images today.

Photos in the stripAdded to the homepageVerdict
8 to 12around 1.2 to 1.8 MBThe sweet spot
24around 3.5 MBNoticeably slower on mobile data
48, both folders fullaround 7 MBAllowed, but a mistake

So eight to twelve per folder is the target. When you have more good photographs than that, the answer is not a longer strip: rotate the strongest ones in and keep the rest in your own archive. A visitor judges the first three they see, not the twentieth.

How to upload, without touching code

The pictures live in the same GitHub repository as the site, which sounds technical but the browser does all of it. You never see any code.

  1. Go to github.com/presencearpitamehta-dotcom/Website-Presence-AM
  2. Click into the images folder, then the folder you need from the table above
  3. Add file (top right), then Upload files
  4. Drag your pictures in, correctly named and sized
  5. Scroll down, write one line saying what you added, click Commit changes

The site rebuilds itself and the pictures are live in about a minute. Replacing works identically: upload a file with the same name and it overwrites the old one.

Upload in batches, not one at a time. Every commit triggers a rebuild, and the hosting plan allows a limited number each month. Five pictures uploaded together is one rebuild. Five uploaded separately is five. Gather them up, then do one upload.

Before you upload a client photo

Not a technical point, but the one with actual consequences.

  • Get written permission for any identifiable client, and say where it will appear. A message saying yes is enough; keep it.
  • Permission is specific. Someone agreeing to a photo in a private report has not agreed to the homepage.
  • Faces are personal data. Your privacy policy covers what you collect through the site, not publishing a client's face, which needs their consent regardless.
  • Keep the original files somewhere safe. The versions on the site are compressed for speed and are not archive copies.

When to send them to me instead: a new picture somewhere that has none today, a new section or page, anything where the shape is wrong and needs cropping decisions, or a batch of client photos you want woven into the testimonial rail alongside quotes. Those need a code change, and they are quick.
