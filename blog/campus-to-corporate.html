The Debut

Campus to corporate: how to arrive like you belong.

By Arpita Mehta · 7 minute read · Updated August 2026

Most graduates spend years preparing for exams and interviews. Almost nobody prepares for what happens after the offer letter arrives. Your reputation at work begins long before your first performance review. It starts on day one: how you walk in, how you introduce yourself, what you ask, and how you respond when you do not know something.

Why first impressions matter more than your degree

Your degree got you the interview. Your presence builds the reputation. Everyone in the building has qualifications. Everyone has completed projects and passed assessments. Very few people know how to create trust, credibility and professional presence on day one. This is where careers begin to separate.

The graduates who become trusted quickly are rarely the ones who know everything. They are the ones who listen carefully, communicate clearly and conduct themselves professionally. People decide whether they trust you long before they decide whether you are talented. That may not seem fair. It is still true.

"Competence gets you hired. Presence gets you remembered."

Five first-day mistakes freshers make

  • Walking in with student habits. Arriving exactly on time, checking your phone, waiting to be told what to do, treating meetings casually. The workplace reads these differently than university does.
  • Speaking before observing. The pressure to prove yourself immediately is real. The people who learn fastest are the ones who watch first: how the team communicates, how meetings run, what gets rewarded.
  • Bluffing instead of admitting uncertainty. "I have not worked with that yet, here is how I would approach it" is more impressive than a confident guess, and far harder to lose credibility with.
  • Not asking a thoughtful question. One good question signals curiosity and judgement. Trying to have all the answers signals the opposite.
  • Switching off once the meeting ends. Assessment does not stop when the conversation does. How you leave the room, follow up and carry yourself afterwards is part of the impression too.

How ready are you, really?

You cannot improve what you cannot see, and how you see yourself is often different from how the room experiences you. The Presence Quotient identifies the behaviours and signals shaping how managers and colleagues perceive you, before you have spoken a word.

Discover Your Presence Quotient

Before your first day

A short readiness check. Be honest with yourself on each one.

  • Have I researched the company?
  • Do I understand my team's role?
  • Can I introduce myself confidently?
  • Do I understand basic workplace etiquette?
  • Have I planned my first week?

Preparation creates confidence. Confidence creates presence. Presence creates opportunity.

Not sure how you will be perceived?

The uncomfortable truth is that most people have no idea how they come across. You see yourself one way. The room sees something else. That difference often decides opportunities, influence and trust.

A fifteen-minute discovery call is the fastest way to talk it through before your next interview, internship or first day. And if you want the full preparation, The Debut is the programme built for exactly this transition.

One day, your degree will matter less. Your presence will matter more.
